# EuRoC Competition Specific Guide

## Competition Overview
The EuRoC (European Rocketry Challenge) is an annual student rocketry competition organized by the Portuguese Space Agency in partnership with the European Space Agency (ESA). It challenges university teams to design, build, and launch rockets to reach specific altitudes (3km or 9km) while carrying a scientific payload. The competition emphasizes safety, innovation, and scientific rigor, with strict adherence to the Student Rocketry Directive (SRD) for all participating teams.

## SRD Structure and Mandatory Requirements
The Student Rocketry Directive (SRD) is the governing document for all EuRoC competitions. It outlines mandatory requirements in the following areas:
- Safety systems (arm/disarm, pressure relief, fragmentation limits)
- Telemetry and tracking (minimum GPS logging, radio frequency compliance)
- Structural integrity (factor of safety, material selection)
- Recovery systems (dual-deployment, landing kinetic energy limits)
- Environmental compliance (noise, debris, launch site restoration)
- Documentation (design reports, test procedures, risk assessments)

All teams must submit SRD compliance documentation during the Technical Evaluation Board review process.

## Technical Evaluation Board Process and Review Criteria
The Technical Evaluation Board (TEB) conducts multi-stage reviews:
1. Preliminary Design Review (PDR): Concept validation and safety approach
2. Critical Design Review (CDR): Detailed design analysis and test plans
3. Flight Readiness Review (FRR): Final verification and launch approval

Review criteria include:
- Safety margin calculations (minimum 2x for critical components)
- Test verification of all safety-critical systems
- Compliance with SRD and ESA safety standards
- Realistic mass properties and trajectory analysis
- Ground support equipment and launch procedures

## Award Structure and Judging Criteria
Awards are given in multiple categories:
- Altitude Achievement (3km and 9km categories)
- Scientific Payload Innovation
- Safety and Professionalism
- Best Design Report
- Team Spirit and Collaboration

Judging criteria combine objective measurements (altitude accuracy, payload performance) with subjective evaluations (documentation quality, safety culture, teamwork).

## Participation Workflow
1. Registration (September-October): Team formation and initial concept submission
2. Preliminary Design Review (November): PDR submission and feedback
3. Critical Design Review (February): CDR submission and design refinement
4. Flight Readiness Review (April): FRR submission and final approvals
5. Launch Campaign (October): Rocket launch and recovery operations
6. Post-Flight Analysis (November): Data submission and reporting

Teams must maintain consistent communication with EuRoC organizers throughout the timeline and adhere to all deadlines for documentation submission.
