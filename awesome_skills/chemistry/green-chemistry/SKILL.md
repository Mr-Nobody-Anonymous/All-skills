# Green Chemistry

## Scope
Green chemistry designs chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate the generation and use of hazardous substances throughout their life cycle.

## The 12 Principles of Green Chemistry (Anastas & Warner)
1. **Prevention**: Prevent waste rather than treat or clean up.
2. **Atom Economy**: Maximize incorporation of all materials into final product.
3. **Less Hazardous Chemical Syntheses**: Generate substances with little/no toxicity.
4. **Designing Safer Chemicals**: Preserve efficacy while minimizing toxicity.
5. **Safer Solvents & Auxiliaries**: Minimize auxiliary substances (replace VOCs with water, supercritical $CO_2$, ionic liquids).
6. **Design for Energy Efficiency**: Run reactions at ambient temperature and pressure.
7. **Use of Renewable Feedstocks**: Agricultural or biomass feedstocks over petrochemicals.
8. **Reduce Derivatives**: Avoid unnecessary derivatization (protecting/deprotecting steps).
9. **Catalysis**: Catalytic reagents ($>1$ turnover) superior to stoichiometric reagents.
10. **Design for Degradation**: Degrade to innocuous environmental products.
11. **Real-time Analysis for Pollution Prevention**: In-process monitoring to prevent hazardous formation.
12. **Inherently Safer Chemistry for Accident Prevention**: Minimize explosion/fire risk.

### Quantitative Green Metrics
- **Atom Economy (AE)**:
  $$\text{AE} = \frac{\text{Molecular Weight of Target Product}}{\sum \text{Molecular Weights of All Reactants}} \times 100\%$$
- **Environmental Factor (E-factor)**:
  $$E = \frac{\text{Total Mass of Waste (kg)}}{\text{Mass of Product (kg)}}$$
