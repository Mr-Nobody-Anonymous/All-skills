# Organizational Psychology (I/O Psychology)

## Scope
Industrial-organizational psychology applies psychological principles to workplace settings, covering personnel selection, motivation, leadership, team dynamics, organizational culture, and change management.

## Motivation Theories

### Content Theories (What Motivates)
- **Maslow's hierarchy**: Physiological → safety → belongingness → esteem → self-actualization
- **Herzberg's two-factor**: Hygiene factors (prevent dissatisfaction: salary, conditions) vs. motivators (drive satisfaction: achievement, growth)
- **Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan)**: Autonomy, competence, relatedness as innate psychological needs

### Process Theories (How Motivation Works)
- **Expectancy theory (Vroom)**: Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence
- **Goal-setting theory (Locke & Latham)**: Specific, challenging goals with feedback maximize performance
- **Equity theory (Adams)**: People compare their input/output ratio to others; inequity creates tension
- **Job characteristics model (Hackman & Oldham)**: Skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, feedback → psychological states → outcomes

## Leadership

### Classical Models
- **Trait theories**: Identify characteristics of effective leaders (intelligence, extraversion, conscientiousness)
- **Behavioral theories**: Task-oriented vs. relationship-oriented behaviors (Ohio State/Michigan studies)
- **Contingency theories**: Effectiveness depends on situation (Fiedler, path-goal, situational leadership)

### Modern Approaches
- **Transformational leadership**: Idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration
- **Leader-member exchange (LMX)**: Quality of dyadic relationships varies within teams
- **Servant leadership**: Leader serves followers' needs first
- **Authentic leadership**: Self-awareness, balanced processing, relational transparency

## Team Dynamics
- **Tuckman's stages**: Forming → storming → norming → performing → adjourning
- **Social loafing**: Reduced effort in groups; mitigated by identifiable contributions
- **Groupthink (Janis)**: Excessive conformity in cohesive groups; prevented by structured dissent
- **Psychological safety (Edmondson)**: Team members feel safe to take interpersonal risks

## Personnel Selection
- **Job analysis**: Task analysis, competency modeling, critical incidents
- **Selection tools**: Structured interviews (most valid), assessment centers, work samples, cognitive ability tests
- **Validity**: Criterion-related (predictive/concurrent), content, construct
- **Adverse impact**: 4/5ths rule, differential prediction, fairness analysis

## Organizational Culture & Change
- **Schein's model**: Artifacts → espoused values → basic assumptions
- **Culture types**: Clan, adhocracy, market, hierarchy (competing values framework)
- **Lewin's change model**: Unfreeze → change → refreeze
- **Kotter's 8-step**: Create urgency → coalition → vision → communicate → empower → quick wins → build → anchor

## Assessment Instruments
- **Job satisfaction**: JDI, MSQ, JDS
- **Engagement**: Utrecht Work Engagement Scale (UWES), Gallup Q12
- **Personality**: Big Five (NEO-PI-R, IPIP), MBTI (limited validity for selection)
- **360-degree feedback**: Multi-source performance assessment
