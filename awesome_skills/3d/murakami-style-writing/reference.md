# Murakami Style — Reference

Condensed from scholarly and critical sources on Haruki Murakami’s (村上春樹) style. Use when you need more detail than in SKILL.md.

## Narrative and Language

- **Empty narrative / transnational style**: Narratives that don’t rely on a single national tradition; language often described as "mukokuseki" (stateless) or "hon'yakuchō" (translation-like), with clear subjects and pronouns.
- **Postmodern and genre mixing**: Mimesis plus genre formulas; noir, fantasy, and daily life combined; reflection on postmodernism within the work.
- **Time and space**: Stories explore self and other, identity; protagonists are both distant from and close to others; cosmopolitan, "neo-modern" tension.

## Sentence and Word Choice

- **Short, simple**: Straightforward syntax and vocabulary; short sentences and clear rhythm. Example: "I didn't say a word, just looked her in the eye and nodded twice."
- **Rhythm**: Murakami treats rhythm as the basis of both music and fiction; rhythm, melody, and harmony are translated into prose structure. His jazz-bar background is part of this.
- **Simile (ように / like)**: Frequent "youni"-type comparisons in Japanese; in English "like", "as if", "as". Pair everyday or abstract X with unexpected image Y (e.g. like misplaced luggage; like a deep-sea creature pretending to be inanimate).

## Narrator and Subject Matter

- **First-person "boku"**: Introspective, slightly detached male first person; often someone who chooses freedom and solitude over intimacy and bonds.
- **Everyday and sensory detail**: Concrete, sometimes trivial daily life (cooking, music, walking, room); stories often start from **memory fragments**—small moments that become symbolic.
- **Loneliness and distance**: Protagonists are lonely and keep distance; the work still creates a "community of loners" for readers.

## Structure and Continuity

- **Single endpoint and repetition**: Sentences with a repetitive form and a single point of arrival; inherited from modern prose and reworked.
- **Across books**: Themes and questions carry from one book to the next; the oeuvre is conceived on a large scale.

## Style Checklist (for generation/rewrite)

| Dimension   | Points                                                                |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sentences   | Short sentences; simple grammar; clear rhythm                         |
| Similes     | Use like / as if; everyday or abstract × unexpected, unsettling       |
| Narrator    | First person; calm, detached; avoid long emotional or moral speeches  |
| Content     | Everyday details (food, music, room, street); memory fragments        |
| Theme       | Loneliness, distance, loss, searching; boundaries between self/other  |
| Tone        | Calm, understated; occasional noir brevity or mystery                 |
| Touchstones | Jazz, classical, coffee, whisky, cats, wells, doors—use sparingly     |
