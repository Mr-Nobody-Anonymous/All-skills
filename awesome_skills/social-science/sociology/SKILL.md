# Sociology

## Scope
Sociology studies human society, social relationships, and social institutions. This skill covers major theoretical perspectives, core concepts, research methods, and key substantive areas.

## Major Theoretical Perspectives

### Structural Functionalism (Durkheim, Parsons, Merton)
- Society is a system of interconnected parts that work together to maintain stability
- **Manifest functions**: Intended and recognized consequences
- **Latent functions**: Unintended and unrecognized consequences
- **Dysfunction**: Element that disrupts social stability
- **Criticism**: Conservative bias, difficulty explaining change

### Conflict Theory (Marx, Weber, Collins)
- Society is characterized by inequality and conflict over scarce resources
- Power, wealth, and status are unequally distributed
- Social change driven by class struggle, group competition
- **Neo-Marxism**: Cultural hegemony (Gramsci), Frankfurt School (Adorno, Horkheimer)
- **Criticism**: Overemphasis on conflict, neglects cooperation and consensus

### Symbolic Interactionism (Mead, Blumer, Goffman)
- Society is constructed through everyday interactions and shared meanings
- **Looking-glass self** (Cooley): Self-concept shaped by how we think others see us
- **Dramaturgy** (Goffman): Social life as theatrical performance (front stage, back stage)
- **Labeling theory**: Deviance is created by social labeling, not inherent in acts
- **Criticism**: Micro-focus, neglects structural constraints

### Contemporary Approaches
- **Poststructuralism** (Foucault): Power/knowledge, discourse, governmentality
- **Bourdieu**: Capital (economic, cultural, social, symbolic), habitus, field
- **Intersectionality** (Crenshaw): Overlapping systems of oppression (race, gender, class)

## Core Concepts

### Socialization
- **Primary** (family): Language, basic norms, identity
- **Secondary** (school, peers, media): Role expectations, cultural values
- **Anticipatory**: Learning norms of a group one aspires to join
- **Resocialization**: Radical alteration of self (total institutions — Goffman)

### Social Stratification
- **Class**: Economic position (income, wealth, occupation)
- **Status**: Social prestige and honor
- **Power**: Ability to impose will despite resistance
- **Social mobility**: Intergenerational (parent-child) and intragenerational (career)
- **Measures**: Gini coefficient (0 = perfect equality, 1 = maximum inequality)

### Culture
- **Material culture**: Physical objects, artifacts, technology
- **Non-material culture**: Beliefs, values, norms, symbols
- **Norms**: Folkways (informal customs) → mores (morally significant) → laws (formal, sanctioned)
- **Cultural relativism vs. ethnocentrism**: Understanding vs. judging other cultures

### Institutions
- Family, education, religion, economy, government, healthcare
- Each has functions, structures, and processes of change

## Research Methods
| Method | Strengths | Limitations |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| Survey | Large samples, generalizability | Response bias, superficial data |
| Interview | Depth, flexibility, lived experience | Time-intensive, interviewer effects |
| Ethnography | Rich contextual understanding | Limited generalizability, researcher bias |
| Content analysis | Unobtrusive, historical data | Interpretation challenges |
| Experiment | Causal inference | Artificial settings, ethical constraints |
| Secondary data | Large-scale, longitudinal | May not fit research question |

### Key Data Sources
- Census data (national statistical agencies)
- General Social Survey (GSS)
- World Values Survey (WVS)
- Panel studies (PSID, BHPS/Understanding Society)
