# Ethics

## Scope
Ethics (moral philosophy) studies questions about what is morally right, good, and just. This skill covers the major normative theories, metaethical foundations, applied ethics, and practical moral reasoning.

## Normative Ethics

### Consequentialism
- **Core principle**: The morality of an action is determined by its consequences
- **Utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill)**: Maximize overall happiness/well-being
  - Act utilitarianism: Evaluate each individual action
  - Rule utilitarianism: Follow rules that generally produce the best outcomes
  - Preference utilitarianism (Singer): Maximize preference satisfaction
- **Objections**: Demandingness, justice problems (sacrificing minorities for majority), difficulty of calculating consequences, integrity objection (Williams)

### Deontology
- **Core principle**: Some actions are intrinsically right or wrong, regardless of consequences
- **Kant's categorical imperative**:
  - Universal law: "Act only according to that maxim by which you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law"
  - Humanity formula: "Treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of another, always as an end and never merely as a means"
- **Rights-based ethics**: Actions are wrong if they violate rights (Locke, Nozick)
- **Ross's prima facie duties**: Fidelity, reparation, gratitude, non-maleficence, beneficence, justice, self-improvement
- **Objections**: Rigidity, conflicting duties, how to derive specific rules

### Virtue Ethics
- **Core principle**: Focus on character and virtues rather than rules or consequences
- **Aristotle**: Virtues are character traits enabling human flourishing (eudaimonia)
  - Doctrine of the mean: Virtue is the mean between excess and deficiency (courage between cowardice and rashness)
  - Phronesis (practical wisdom): The master virtue that guides application of others
- **Virtues**: Justice, courage, temperance, generosity, honesty, compassion, integrity
- **Objections**: Cultural relativism of virtues, action-guidance problem, self-centeredness

### Care Ethics
- **Core principle**: Morality grounded in relationships and responsiveness to others' needs
- **Key thinkers**: Gilligan, Noddings, Held
- **Emphasis**: Particular relationships over abstract principles, empathy, contextual judgment

## Metaethics

### Moral Realism vs. Anti-Realism
- **Moral realism**: Objective moral facts exist independently of human beliefs
- **Moral anti-realism**: No objective moral facts
  - Error theory (Mackie): Moral statements are all false
  - Expressivism (Ayer, Blackburn): Moral statements express attitudes, not beliefs
  - Constructivism (Rawls, Scanlon): Moral facts are constructed through rational agreement

### Is-Ought Problem (Hume)
Cannot derive normative conclusions from purely descriptive premises without normative premises.

## Applied Ethics
- **Bioethics**: Abortion, euthanasia, genetic engineering, organ allocation, informed consent
- **Business ethics**: Corporate responsibility, whistleblowing, fair wages, environmental impact
- **Technology ethics**: AI bias, surveillance, privacy, autonomous weapons, digital rights
- **Environmental ethics**: Animal rights, sustainability, climate justice, wilderness preservation
- **Professional ethics**: Codes of conduct for medicine, law, engineering, journalism

## Moral Reasoning Framework
1. **Identify the ethical issue**: What moral question is at stake?
2. **Gather relevant facts**: Who is affected? What are the consequences? What rights are involved?
3. **Apply ethical frameworks**: What does each major theory recommend?
4. **Consider stakeholders**: Whose interests matter? Who is most vulnerable?
5. **Evaluate options**: Weigh competing considerations
6. **Make and justify a decision**: Provide clear moral reasoning
7. **Reflect**: Would you be comfortable if your reasoning were public?
